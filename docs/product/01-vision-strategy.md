# Vision & Strategy
**Product:** FirsThing Platform · **Phase:** 1 — Vision & Strategy · **Status:** Approved
**Last updated:** 2026-08-10 · **Mode:** Ecosystem

---

## 1. Problem statement

A residential society's common-area electricity load — lighting, water pumps, eventually solar
and wastewater — is expensive, invisible, and inefficient, and fixing it requires capital most
societies won't spend. Today, without FirsThing, the answer is nothing: no ongoing efficiency
measure, no verification of whether existing equipment runs efficiently, and no path forward
short of the committee approving a large upfront capex project themselves (LED retrofit, pump
automation, solar) — a decision residential committees are structurally bad at forcing through
their own residents.

FirsThing removes the capital barrier: it installs and owns the lighting fully, and the pump
automation layer (motor controllers, VFDs, contactors, pressure switches, water-level sensors,
actuator valves) partially, at its own expense, then earns back that investment as a share of the
verified savings it produces — the society pays only from money it wasn't spending before, not
from a new bill. This is a genuine wedge: there is currently no other company in this market doing
continuous per-circuit metered verification paired with a zero-upfront revenue-share model. The
two nearest adjacent categories — traditional ESCOs (one-time-audited, guaranteed-savings
contracts with no ongoing verification) and common-area solar vendors (generation-focused,
net-metering, no lighting/pump savings-share mechanism) — solve a different problem in a different
way.

The commercial mechanism itself is currently unenforced by software: a benchmark is set per
circuit at signing, and each month's metered performance either falls within ±5% (society billed
the fixed amount) or outside it (FirsThing investigates and, if unfixable, adjusts the bill on the
same percentage-share basis). Today this reconciliation, along with everything downstream of it —
which society is even active, what it's owed, what's been inspected, what's broken — happens by
hand. The application built to support this has accreted screens without a settled model of the
business it serves, and its own design-handoff prototype and its shipped code now disagree about
what five of six admin modules should even do.

## 2. Vision

A society signs one contract and never has to think about their common-area electricity again.
FirsThing installs, owns, and monitors the lighting and pump automation; every month, one number
— verified savings, in rupees — lands in the society's account view, generated by the platform
from metered readings against a versioned, auditable benchmark, not assembled by hand. The
society's committee logs in and sees cumulative savings and generation across every service line
FirsThing runs for them, with the underlying contract terms and current benchmark visible and
traceable. FirsThing's own operations run the same way in reverse: ops sees every society's
health at a glance — who's off-band, who has an open ticket, whose inspection is due — and an
inspector in the field sees exactly what work is assigned to them, updates it from their phone,
and never has to separately report what they've done.

## 4. Target outcome & success metrics

**North star:** ₹ of verified monthly savings delivered across the portfolio, computed and
attested by the platform (not reconstructed after the fact) — baseline unmeasured today (the
number exists only in ad hoc spreadsheets), target: 100% of active societies' monthly savings
figure is system-generated by MVP, ₹ volume tracked from GOAL-01's ship date onward.

| Metric | Type | Baseline | Target | Instrument | Owner |
|--------|------|----------|--------|-----------|-------|
| ₹ verified monthly savings delivered, portfolio-wide | north star | unmeasured (manual today) | 100% system-generated by MVP; ₹ volume grows with society count | Sum of monthly billing-decision records (GOAL-01) | Yugesh |
| % societies with zero escalated billing disputes in a given month | supporting | unmeasured | to be baselined once GOAL-01 ships, then held flat or improved as society count grows | Count of disputed/held invoices ÷ active societies, per month | Yugesh / ops |
| % of monthly bills generated as system-of-record with no manual reconciliation | supporting | 0% (fully manual today) | 100% by MVP | Derived from whether a bill's billing-decision record exists vs. was hand-entered | Yugesh |
| Avg days to close a fixable/not-fixable review (OQ-02) | supporting | unmeasured | baseline once tracked, then watch for drift as portfolio grows | Timestamp delta on the review record (INV-03 mechanism, designed in Phase 3) | ops |
| Billing disputes / payments-on-hold count, and open ticket/inspection backlog size | counter | unmeasured | watched, not targeted — a rising trend while the north star also rises is the failure signal (CON-01's "growth without control" risk) | Count of held invoices; count of open tickets past SLA; count of not-fixable adjustments, flagged if the >±15% (severe deviation) rate rises | ops |

## 5. Positioning

No company was identified doing the same combination of continuous per-circuit metered
verification and a zero-upfront revenue-share model for residential-society common-area loads in
India — the two nearest reference points solve a related but different problem:

| Alternative | How it solves the problem today | Where it falls short | Our answer |
|-------------|--------------------------------|---------------------|-----------|
| Do nothing | Society pays whatever the inefficient bill is | No visibility, no path to improvement without capex the committee won't approve | FirsThing takes on the capex risk; society pays only from realized savings |
| Traditional ESCO (performance contracting) | One-time energy audit, guaranteed-savings contract, retrofit installed and financed by the client or a third party | Savings are asserted at contract signing, not continuously re-verified — no ongoing metered proof month to month | FirsThing meters and re-verifies every month, per circuit, and adjusts the bill itself when reality diverges from the benchmark |
| Common-area solar vendor | Rooftop/common-area solar, often group net-metered, sold or financed to the society | Generation-focused only — doesn't touch lighting/pump *efficiency*, no ongoing shared-savings billing mechanism | Solar is modeled as a distinct, separately-billed service line (SVC-03) alongside efficiency, not a replacement for it |

**The wedge:** two parts, not one — continuous per-circuit metered verification is the technical
wedge; FirsThing bearing the capex so the society bears none is the commercial wedge. Neither
alone is the moat; together they are what "do nothing" and both adjacent categories fail to offer.

## 7. Business model

Revenue today is purely the ongoing savings-share fee: FirsThing installs and owns lighting fully
and pump automation hardware partially, at its own capital cost, and recoups it as a percentage of
the verified monthly savings it produces, with **zero upfront cost to the society** — this
zero-investment framing is itself half the commercial wedge (§5). A **security deposit** from the
society is a planned future addition (CON-09), intended as collateral against payment holds and
disputes rather than a capex charge — worth keeping messaging-distinct from the "zero investment"
pitch when it's built, since a refundable deposit and a hardware purchase are not the same thing to
a committee deciding whether to sign. Not yet scoped into this blueprint's build (Phase 3 decides
whether/when).

Solar (SVC-03) is billed on a separate basis from the lighting/pump savings-share number (OQ-05,
resolved) — the exact solar commercial model is not yet defined and stays out of this blueprint's
detailed scope beyond data-model accommodation (CON-03).
