# Build the founders' "late first" operations page

The founders' dashboard should open with **one never-collapsed list of what is late**, then today's and this week's meetings, then one compact card per domain (payments, meters, installations, complaints, demos), with every number linking to the filtered list behind it. That is the action-first layout small field-service tools use, and it matches the request better than a chart-led overview. About two thirds of the data is already loaded by the existing Portfolio page (`/admin`): meter alerts, below-band circuits, the notification feed, and the "Coming meetings" query. Five pieces are missing. They are rupee totals for received, outstanding and overdue payments; a portfolio-wide installation board; a portfolio-wide demo board; a complaints view that includes in-progress tickets; and a single rule for what "late" means. The last is the real design work. Only six kinds of item carry a deadline today: invoices, assigned tasks and booked visits, installation days, demo result reviews, monthly inspections, and gate passes. Complaints, meter alerts, deal stages and most demo steps have no deadline at all, so the founders must choose thresholds before the page can call them late honestly. The recommended build is a new route gated by a new `view_founder_dashboard` permission, three new `cache()`-wrapped loaders, and one pure `src/lib/lateness.ts` module that holds every threshold in a written table with tests.

## Comparable products lead with an attention list, not charts

Owner-facing operations tools fall into two shapes. The **action-first** shape opens on what needs the owner. Jobber's Home gives admins "a to-do list of the tasks that require your attention" beside the day's appointments ([Jobber help](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/home-in-the-jobber-app/)), and its web Home names the **top three overdue clients, how much each owes and how much is late, in red**, linking to the full balance summary ([Jobber help: Home](https://help.getjobber.com/es/articles/home/)). The **snapshot-first** shape opens on today's metrics: ServiceTitan defaults to the current day's revenue, bookings and conversion, with an info icon defining each KPI ([ServiceTitan](https://help.servicetitan.com/roofing/docs/dashboard-business-metrics)), and Razorpay stacks alerts, balance, collections and last settlement ([Razorpay docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/dashboard/razorpay-home)). A two-person company asking "what is not on time" is squarely the first case.

Lateness itself is shown in three recurring ways. Freshdesk puts a **count ribbon** across the top (Unresolved, Overdue, Due today, Open, On hold, Unassigned) ([Freshdesk](https://support.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/37556-checking-recent-helpdesk-activity)). Zendesk-style views sort a **countdown column ascending** so the next breach is on top, and Geckoboard's Zendesk example pairs a "Nearing SLA breach" table with an "Active breached" number, because "about to be late" and "already late" are different questions ([Geckoboard](https://www.geckoboard.com/dashboard-examples/itsm/zendesk-sla-backlog-dashboard/)). Salesforce Field Service gives dispatchers an "In Jeopardy" filter as their first priority and marks broken rules with a small triangle that names the rule on hover ([Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/field-service-dispatcher-console-for-dispatchers/manage-service-appointments)). One negative example matters as much: Asana's "Past due" group **starts collapsed**, and users complain it hides exactly the work they needed to see ([Asana forum](https://forum.asana.com/t/my-tasks-list-when-sorted-by-due-date-has-past-due-collapsed-as-default-would-prefer-it-expanded/453160)). No product found merges payments, devices, tickets and projects into one late list; Jobber's mixed to-do list is the closest precedent, so FirsThing's cross-domain list is a synthesis, not a copy.

Each domain has a well-worn pattern of its own. For money, **Zoho Invoice — which FirsThing already uses — shows Total Receivables as Current and Overdue, each clicking through to an aging report** with default buckets of Current, 1–15, 16–30, 31–45 and over 45 days ([Zoho Invoice help](https://www.zoho.com/invoice/help/reports/receivable.html)); Chargebee adds net overdue by bucket and ages from each invoice's due date ([Chargebee](https://chargebee.com/docs/receivables/dashboard.html)). For devices, fleet monitors lead with a single "all is well" indicator and then a sortable list of sites needing attention, as Enphase and SolarEdge market it ([Enphase feature sheet](https://www.solarelectricsupply.com/media/sparsh/product_attachment/custom/upload/Enlighten_feature_sheet.pdf); [SolarEdge](https://solaredge.maglr.com/solaredge-software-suite/monitoring)). For pipelines, HubSpot is criticised because "time in stage" only exists after a deal leaves the stage ([HubSpot community](https://community.hubspot.com/t5/Dashboards-Reporting/Time-in-current-deal-stage/m-p/296432/highlight/true)), while Pipedrive marks a deal "rotting" after a per-stage idle period and, notably, **does not let a far-future scheduled activity stop it rotting** ([Pipedrive](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/the-rotting-feature)). No product documents a pattern for tracking pilots or demos.

The design authorities agree on the frame. Stephen Few defines a dashboard as information that "fits entirely on a single computer screen so it can be monitored at a glance" and lists inadequate context and ineffective highlighting among the common failures ([UXmatters review](https://uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2007/04/book-review-information-dashboard-design.php)). NN/g separates time-sensitive operational dashboards from analytical ones ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/)). PatternFly says each card should carry one metric or a tight group, and "never use a card to show a dense amount of repetitive, related content" — use a table ([PatternFly](https://patternfly.org/patterns/dashboard/design-guidelines)). GOV.UK's performance platform required every module to map to "a decision or an action" ([GDS blog](https://gds.blog.gov.uk/2012/10/23/performance-platform-beta/)). These line up with rules the app already holds: only warn and bad numbers are tinted, because "a green number carries no information the absence of amber does not already carry" ([src/components/ui.tsx](../../../src/components/ui.tsx)).

## Portfolio already loads most of it; five pieces are missing

The existing Portfolio page runs **13 reads in one `Promise.all`** and already renders the Meters card, the "Below the agreed band" card, a "Needs you" list built from the shared notification feed, and — through `YourTasks` — the founders' own tasks and **meetings in the next seven days they host or are invited to, with a Join link** ([src/app/admin/page.tsx](../../../src/app/admin/page.tsx); [src/app/admin/your-tasks.tsx](../../../src/app/admin/your-tasks.tsx)). Meetings are therefore solved: they are `ScheduledEvent` rows of kind `meeting`, synced both ways with Google Calendar, and the query can be lifted out unchanged, perhaps widened past four items.

Meters are also nearly solved. `MeterAlert` carries every kind the founders mean by "live metering issues" — offline, out of range, savings out of band, and the three demo-monitoring kinds — with `openedAt`, `acknowledgedAt`, `raiseCount` and `closedAt` ([prisma/schema.prisma](../../../prisma/schema.prisma)). Offline is already declared after **two consecutive missed hourly polls**, which matches vendor guidance to wait two to three reporting intervals before calling a device offline ([Schneider Electric community](https://community.se.com/t5/Building-Automation-Knowledge/Offline-monitoring-of-a-Zigbee-device-in-EBO/ta-p/385960); [OneUptime](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-03-31-redis-iot-device-heartbeat-monitoring/view)). A founders' card can read `meterAlert.findMany({ closedAt: null })` directly and skip the heavier `allMeterRows()`, which also builds hourly spans and sparklines ([src/lib/meter-view.ts](../../../src/lib/meter-view.ts)).

The gaps are concrete:

| Ask | What exists | What is missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payments received / outstanding / overdue | `BillingInvoice` with `dueDate` and CON-13 status; `Payment` with amount and TDS; `settledTotal()` ([src/lib/payment.ts](../../../src/lib/payment.ts)) | No portfolio rupee totals; the feed lists overdue societies but sums nothing |
| Installation status | Projects, planned days, batches, blockers, certificate; `evaluateDayGate()` returns `blocked`, `at_risk`, `late_approved` ([src/lib/installation-gate.ts](../../../src/lib/installation-gate.ts)) | Computed only per deal on `/admin/pipeline/[id]/installation`; no portfolio loader |
| Demo status | `/admin/demo-monitoring` loads every live demo and its step; review SLA of 24 h ([src/lib/demo-result-review.ts](../../../src/lib/demo-result-review.ts)) | Gated to field or pipeline permissions; no summary or step-age view |
| All complaints | `Ticket` desk with status counts ([src/app/admin/tickets/page.tsx](../../../src/app/admin/tickets/page.tsx)) | Feed carries only `open` tickets, deliberately omitting `in_progress`; no deadline field |
| What is not on time | Deadlines on invoices, tasks, CON-21 days, demo reviews, inspections, gate passes | No shared rule; no deadline on tickets, meter alerts, deal stages or demo steps |

Two traps sit in the payment data. First, **defaulter societies are removed from `openNotifications()`** and moved to a separate list, so a payment view built on the main feed would silently omit the worst overdue invoices ([src/lib/notifications.ts](../../../src/lib/notifications.ts)). Second, the arrears sweep marks an invoice `overdue` only **two days after its due date** (`OVERDUE_TRACKING_AFTER_DAYS = 2`, [src/lib/arrears.ts](../../../src/lib/arrears.ts)), while a founder reading "overdue" means "past due". The money card should age every unpaid released invoice from its own due date and show the CON-13 phase (overdue, warning, suspended) as a separate line, so the two meanings never share one number. Retail invoices live in a different table (`RetailInvoice`), and whether they belong in the totals is a founder decision; the default should be a separate small line rather than silent inclusion.

## Only six item types have a real deadline today

A late list is only honest if each row breaks a written rule. For six item types the rule already exists in code; for the rest, the founders must set a threshold, and the report proposes one grounded in industry or Indian practice. The proposals below are design choices, labelled as such.

| Item | "Late" means | Source of the rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invoice | Unpaid after its due date (received + TDS short of total); amber 1–15 days, red beyond, plus a red marker past 45 days from issue | Existing `dueDate`; 45 days mirrors MSMED Act s.15 ([TaxGuru](https://www.taxguru.in/income-tax/section-43bh-msme-payment.html)) |
| Task, survey visit, replacement day, demo meeting | Still `scheduled` after its day | Existing `taskState()` ([src/lib/tasks.ts](../../../src/lib/tasks.ts)) |
| Installation day | Planned day passed with no batch, a day gate `blocked`/`at_risk`, or a disputed batch | Existing CON-21 gate |
| Demo result review | Open more than 24 h | Existing `REVIEW_SLA_HOURS` |
| Monthly inspection | Last month's inspection not finalised | Existing reminder |
| Gate pass | Unsubmitted by the stage it gates | Existing ADR-006 sweep |
| Complaint ticket | **Proposed:** not taken up within 1 business day; not resolved within 5 business days (24–48 h when common-area lights are out) | Design choice; India's e-commerce rules use 48 h to acknowledge and one month to redress ([ONDC handbook](https://www.ondc.org/legal-compliance-handbook/ANNEXURE-–-E.1086291982.html)); facilities SLAs run 30 min–2 h response ([Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/facility-management/facility-vendor-sla-tracking-response-time-cmms-guide)) |
| Meter alert | **Proposed:** offline over 48 h, out of range over 24 h, unacknowledged after 4 business hours | Design choice; tiered offline alarms escalate as outages persist ([ThingsBoard](https://thingsboard.io/iot-hub/alarm-rules/device-offline-alarm/)) |
| Demo step | **Proposed:** a baseline or post window open more than 7 days amber, 10 days red; a booked replacement day 2 days past | Design choice from the five-valid-day windows |
| Deal stage | **Proposed:** idle in a stage more than N days, N set per stage | Pipedrive-style; needs a stored stage-entered date |

Three rules from mature tools should be adopted wholesale. **Acknowledging is not resolving**: PagerDuty stops escalation on acknowledge but leaves the incident open ([PagerDuty](https://support.pagerduty.com/v1/docs/escalation-policies)), and the app already keeps those states apart for meter alerts. **Clocks do not pause silently**: Jira Service Management pauses only on explicit conditions ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira/kb/understanding-why-an-sla-is-paused-in-a-jira-service-management-ticket/)), so a ticket clock may stop only in an explicit "waiting on society" state. **Thresholds are fixed and versioned**, never edited to turn something green; the Institute of Project Management calls moving them to avoid red a governance failure ([IPM](https://instituteprojectmanagement.com/blog/rag-status-in-project-management/)).

Two of the proposals need schema work to be exact. Tickets have no `dueAt`; age from `createdAt` and `assignedAt` is enough for a fixed per-type grid, so a field is only needed if per-ticket deadlines are wanted. Deal stages have no stage-entered timestamp, and `Pipeline.updatedAt` changes on any edit, so stage ageing either waits for a small migration that stamps `stageEnteredAt` or is left off the first version. Leaving it off is the better call: a deal list that is half-accurate trains the founders to ignore the whole late list. The 45-day marker is also conditional — it carries legal weight only if FirsThing holds Udyam micro or small registration, and whether a housing society counts as a "buyer" under the MSMED Act is a question for counsel, so the screen should call it "past 45 days" rather than imply a legal claim.

## The page: a late list, a meeting strip and five cards

**Route and access.** Build a new page, `/admin/founders`, rather than extending Portfolio. Portfolio is every admin's landing page and already runs 13 queries; the founders' page adds payment aggregates, an installation board and demo facts, which nobody else needs to pay for. Gate it with a new `AdminPermission` value, `view_founder_dashboard`, granted to Manish and Yogesh through `/admin/users`, checked in `requireAdminPage(...)` and in the sidebar's `show*` filter ([src/lib/admin-permissions.ts](../../../src/lib/admin-permissions.ts); [src/components/app-shell.tsx](../../../src/components/app-shell.tsx)). This follows the `approve_showcase` precedent and avoids a second hard-wired email like `isSuperAdmin`, whose own comment says it is "not a general pattern" ([src/lib/super-admin.ts](../../../src/lib/super-admin.ts)). The page will show billing, meter and ticket data whose own pages have narrower gates; that should be recorded as a deliberate decision. Manish's admin account cannot be confirmed from the repository, because accounts live in the gitignored `restore/`.

**Layout, top to bottom.** A `PageHeader` with a chip counting late items. Then a `StatRow` summary strip in words — "3 payments overdue · 1 meter down 3 days · 2 installation days unapproved" — where each count jumps to its rows. Then the **Not on time** table, expanded and never collapsed, sorted by severity and then by how late. Every row uses one grammar: the item type as an icon *and* a word; the society and item; the clock in words ("Overdue by 4 days", "Day 9 of 5", "Due in 3 h") rather than JSM's negative numbers, which suit trained agents, not founders; a red or amber chip with a word; a **named owner**; and the next action as a verb linking to the screen where it happens. Repeats collapse to one row per society per type ("Ace City: 3 invoices overdue, oldest 72 days"), the rule the app already learned when 14 identical inspection reminders made the bell meaningless. Below it, a meetings strip lifted from `YourTasks`. Then a 7/5 grid of cards, money first as Portfolio already orders it:

- **Payments:** received this month, outstanding, and overdue in rupees; a stacked aging bar (Not yet due, 1–15, 16–30, 31–45, over 45) that doubles as a printed figure; the top five overdue societies with amounts, defaulters included and marked; a link to the release queue for invoices not yet released. The buckets match Zoho's defaults, so the founders can cross-check against the source system, and the 45-day boundary coincides with the MSMED line. Skip DSO: across about fifteen billing societies it is too noisy to read, and it hides a long tail unless shown beside the buckets ([Stuut](https://www.stuut.ai/blog/dso-vs-ar-aging-report)).
- **Meters:** one health line ("41 reporting, 2 offline"), then open alerts by kind with their age and owner.
- **Installations:** each active project with installed against contracted lights, its next planned day, and any `blocked`, `at_risk` or `late_approved` day.
- **Complaints:** open and in-progress tickets of type complaint and device replacement, each with age; enquiries kept out of the count.
- **Demos:** counts per step, plus demos past their step threshold and reviews past 24 h.

Only late figures are tinted; healthy figures stay plain text. Every card follows INV-06's loading, empty, error and degraded states, all dates go through `src/lib/format-date.ts`, and grid children carry `min-w-0` so the page collapses to one column on a phone without sideways scroll ([src/components/ui.tsx](../../../src/components/ui.tsx)).

**Implementation.** Add one pure module, `src/lib/lateness.ts`, holding the rule table above and exporting a function per item type that returns `{ state: "ok" | "amber" | "red", clock, reason }`, with a matching `tests/lateness.test.ts` — the same shape as `release-triage.ts` and `demo-result-review.ts`. Add three loaders, each `cache()`-wrapped: `payment-summary` (a grouped query over released non-voided invoices and their payments, using `settledTotal()` so TDS counts toward settlement), `installation-board` (projects not complete, with planned days and batches, feeding `evaluateDayGateChain()` unchanged), and `demo-board` (lifting the demo-monitoring query into a shared loader). The late list is then a merge of those loaders plus `openNotifications()` — which costs nothing extra, because the layout's bell already computes it once per request — plus `defaulterNotifications()`, so nothing is lost to the split. Wrap the slower cards in `<Suspense>` so the late list and meetings render first. Nothing in `src/` caches across requests today, and the root layout forces dynamic rendering, so cross-request caching with `unstable_cache` should wait for a Research Gate check against `node_modules/next/dist/docs/`. Query costs here are judged from query shape, not measured, so a first deploy should log render times before optimising.

**Notifications.** Keep the late list an indicator on the page, not a stream of pushes. NN/g distinguishes indicators on an item from notifications about events ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/indicators-validations-notifications)), and alert fatigue shows up as rising volume without rising incidents ([Rootly](https://rootly.com/alert-management/alert-fatigue.md)). The founders should be pushed only when an item breaches its resolve threshold or threatens a bill, with the owner and the operations lead hearing about it first.

## Conclusion

The founders are not short of data; they are short of a definition. Every domain they named already has a home in the schema, and most already have a screen, but "not on time" is answered six different ways in six places and not at all for complaints, meter fixes and deal stages. Writing one threshold table, in code, with tests, is the decision that makes the page trustworthy, and it improves the rest of the product too: the same rules can later drive the ticket desk, the meter list and the notification bell, so a ticket cannot be late on the founders' page and fine on the support desk.

The decisions to bring to Manish and Yogesh before building are short: whether to accept the proposed complaint and meter thresholds; whether retail invoices count in the payment totals; whether "my meetings" means their own or everyone's; and whether deal-stage ageing is worth a migration now or waits for version two. Everything else follows from patterns this app already uses.
