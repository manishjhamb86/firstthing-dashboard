# Comparable products: how companies publicly rate, rank, highlight or name their own customers/counterparties

Method note: research ran on 2026-10-21 via web search. Every direct page fetch failed with DNS errors (adda.io, textileexcellence.com, verdictum.in, gov.uk), so the findings below rest on search-result summaries of the cited pages, not on full-page reads. Several sources are dated (2016–2022) and are flagged as such. Treat the vendor blog claims (MyGate, ADDA, Upwork) as self-reported.

## Two-sided platforms where the provider rates the customer (Uber, Airbnb, Upwork/Fiverr, Urban Company, Swiggy/Zomato)

### Takeaway
On the major platforms the provider does rate the customer, but a bad customer is almost never publicly named to the world. The rating is shown to the customer themselves and to future providers inside the platform. Penalties are private (warnings, then loss of access), and the platforms publish no "worst customers" list. The one public-by-design exception is Airbnb's two-way reviews, which are tied to a real stay, published double-blind, and answerable by the person reviewed.

### Cited Findings
- **Uber: rider ratings since 2019.** Drivers rate riders 1–5 stars. A rider sees their own rating under their name in the app menu. In May 2019 Uber began deactivating riders whose rating falls well below their city's average, first in the US and Canada, under refreshed community guidelines. — [Press Herald, 2019](https://www.pressherald.com/2019/05/29/uber-will-ban-passengers-with-low-ratings/); [WEF, 2019](https://weforum.org/stories/2019/05/uber-will-start-deactivating-riders-with-low-ratings)
- **Uber: warnings before removal, and the threshold kept private.** Uber did not publish the rider threshold. It said any rider at risk "will receive several notifications and opportunities to improve" before losing access, and it gave tips to improve: be polite to drivers, don't leave trash, don't ask drivers to speed. Only "a handful" of riders lost access in the first year. — [WEF, 2019](https://weforum.org/agenda/2019/05/uber-will-start-deactivating-riders-with-low-ratings); [Citizen Digital](https://www.citizen.digital/news/uber-now-banning-passengers-low-ratings-drivers-250394) (dated, 2019)
- **Uber: the guidelines apply to both sides.** Breaching them can lose a rider or driver access, temporarily or permanently. In Australia and New Zealand the loss covers all Uber apps. — [Uber AU newsroom](https://www.uber.com/en-AU/newsroom/safetyandrespectau); [Uber deactivation policy](https://www.uber.com/pr/en/legal/deactivation-policy/)
- **Airbnb: timing and visibility of two-way reviews.**
  - Each side has 14 days after checkout to review.
  - Reviews publish when both are in or the window closes, so neither side can retaliate after reading the other's (double-blind).
  - Anyone can read the public written reviews and overall stars.
  - Subcategory ratings and private notes are visible only to the host and Airbnb.

  — [Airbnb Help: how reviews work](https://www.airbnb.co.in/help/article/13/how-do-reviews-work); [Hostfully, 2026](https://www.hostfully.com/blog/airbnb-review-policy/)
- **Airbnb: moderation and right of reply.**
  - Reviews are not pre-moderated, but must relate to a real reservation and can be removed for policy violations.
  - The author can ask for removal within 30 days.
  - The person reviewed can post a public response.
  - A community answer (unofficial) says removals are rare.

  — [Airbnb Help article 2825](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2825); [Airbnb community FAQ](https://community.withairbnb.com/t5/Host-guides/All-About-Reviews-FAQs/m-p/1551528)
- **Upwork: client profiles carry signals for freelancers.** A client profile shows "Payment method is verified" once billing is verified, plus completed jobs, star ratings and freelancers' written feedback on the client. A job with no feedback shows "No feedback given". The point is to help freelancers judge clients before applying. — [Upwork: client profiles](https://www.upwork.com/resources/upwork-client-profiles-what-you-need-to-know)
- **Upwork: a conflicting definition.** A third-party blog says "payment verified" means milestone payments were released. That contradicts Upwork's own definition (a verified billing method), so Upwork's should be preferred. — [websitebuilderinsider](https://www.websitebuilderinsider.com/what-does-payment-verified-mean-on-upwork/)
- **Urban Company: partners rate customers, little else known.** A roughly 2021 company blog post mentions a new screen where partners can rate customers. No public policy on visibility or consequences was found. Customers' ratings of professionals are public (4.82/5 average in FY23). — [Urban Company blog](https://www.urbancompany.com/blog/?p=43095); [UC FY23 summary](https://www.urbancompany.com/blog/urban-companys-annual-business-summary-fy23)

### Inferences
- Platforms keep customer-side ratings mostly private. The rating informs only the counterparty about to transact and the customer themselves, which is the "awareness" effect FirsThing wants without publishing a society's name to everyone.
- Uber's sequence (private score → several warnings with improvement tips → removal of access, with the threshold kept private) is a direct template for a "you are at risk of being listed" notice before any public listing.
- Airbnb's safeguards translate to a society listing:
  - every rating is tied to a real transaction (a specific invoice or visit);
  - a time-boxed window;
  - the rated party can see its rating and reply;
  - removal for policy violations.
- None of these platforms publishes a "worst customers" list. A public defaulter wall would go further than any mainstream two-sided platform.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for how Swiggy, Zomato, Ola or Rapido rate customers, or whether those ratings are visible. The searches returned nothing usable.
- Fiverr's buyer-rating mechanics were not researched (the call budget ran out).
- Uber's current (2025–26) India rider-rating policy was not confirmed; the sources are 2019.
- Urban Company's customer-rating visibility and consequences are undocumented publicly.

## B2B payment-behaviour and defaulter disclosure (credit bureaus, MSME Samadhaan, trade-association lists, RBI wilful defaulters)

### Takeaway
India has a spectrum of mechanisms:
- **Confidential scoring**: the CIBIL MSME Rank, shown to lenders.
- **Government delayed-payment registries that do not name buyers publicly**: MSME Samadhaan, now replaced by the ODR portal.
- **Private trade-body defaulter lists**: shown to members, after verification.
- **The RBI wilful-defaulter regime**, the most formal: a two-committee process, a hearing for the borrower, and a board-approved policy before any photo is published.

Every legitimate public naming scheme has a threshold, verification, a chance for the named party to respond or pay, and a removal path.

### Cited Findings
- **CIBIL MSME Rank (CMR): what it is.** A 1–10 commercial risk rank from TransUnion CIBIL for MSMEs; 1 is the lowest risk. It estimates the probability of becoming an NPA. — [Bajaj Finserv](https://bajajfinserv.in/what-is-cibil-msme-rank-and-its-key-parameters); [IIFL](https://www.iifl.com/blogs/business-loan/understanding-cmr-cibil-msme-rank-and-its-impact-on-interest-rates)
- **CIBIL MSME Rank (CMR): what feeds it.** Inputs include payment timeliness, amount overdue, delinquency status, liquidity and credit-history length. One guide bands it CMR 1–3 low, 4–7 medium, 8–10 high risk. It is shown to lenders, who use it as one input, not to the public. — [Muthoot Finance](https://www.muthootfinance.com/blog/what-is-cmr-understanding-cibil-msme-rank-and-its-importance); [Lendingkart](https://www.lendingkart.com/blog/cibil-msme-rank-meaning-and-benefits/)
- **MSME Samadhaan does not publicly name defaulting buyers.** A ClearTax article (around 2021–22) contrasts it with NoDefaulters, which does. — [ClearTax](https://news.cleartax.in/cimsme-launches-portal-for-reporting-payment-defaults-to-msmes/7536/amp)
- **MSME Samadhaan: scale and closure.**
  - It reportedly stopped taking new references from 15 Oct 2025; suppliers are redirected to the ODR portal.
  - A cited Sept 2026 analysis counts 1,48,697 references.
  - The legal basis is the MSMED Act: a buyer paying an MSE supplier beyond 45 days can be taken to the MSE Facilitation Council.
  - The closure and the reference count are single-source and unverified.

  — [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/?p=130194); [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/payments-micro-and-small-enterprises/)
- **NoDefaulters.com (run by CIMSME, an MSME body): listing process.** A buyer is contacted first and given an agreed period to pay. Only then is it listed as a defaulter, visible to registered users, not the public. The buyer can respond, and conciliation or arbitration is offered. — [ClearTax](https://news.cleartax.in/cimsme-launches-portal-for-reporting-payment-defaults-to-msmes/7536/amp)
- **NoDefaulters.com: early figures (around 2021–22).** 365 registered users, 127 of whom had reported about ₹54.2 crore in defaults. The current status is unverified. — [ClearTax](https://news.cleartax.in/?p=7536)
- **FOSTTA (Federation of Surat Textile Traders Associations): listing mechanics.** It announced it would name distributors defaulting beyond a 180-day credit period, verifying claims before listing them on its mobile app. — [Textile Excellence](https://textileexcellence.com/single-news/6697/payment-crisis-surat-based-fostta-to-list-defaulters-publicly); [Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/?p=257160) (date not confirmed)
- **FOSTTA: early volume.** The app drew over 3,000 complaints worth about ₹170 crore within a month, with over 70% of the accused firms from other states. — same sources
- **Maskati Kapad Market Mahajan (Ahmedabad fabric traders): blacklisting.** It blacklisted about 1,924 traders and 1,047 agents nationally (786 traders and 412 agents from Ahmedabad) after members complained of delayed settlements. It also acted against agents who sourced new suppliers for the blacklisted. — [search summary of a trade-press report](https://apparelresources.com/?p=297705) (undated; the article was not opened)
- **Ahmedabad police SIT.** A police Special Investigation Team investigated 1,277 cheating cases involving traders and 677 involving agents alongside the blacklist. — same source
- **ICA defaulters list (international precedent).** The International Cotton Association in London keeps a public defaulters list. 84 Pakistani mills were added in Dec 2024, and India had 89 listed companies on a 2012-era list. — [ProPakistani 2024](https://propakistani.pk/2024/12/10/84-pakistani-textile-mills-added-to-defaulters-list/); [Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/node/1128367) (older)
- **RBI wilful defaulters (Master Directions of 30 July 2024): the process.**
  - A threshold of outstanding dues of ₹25 lakh or more.
  - An Identification Committee decides first; a Review Committee then takes written representations from the borrower "in the interest of natural justice".
  - Photos may be published only under a board-approved, non-discriminatory policy, and only of those declared wilful defaulters through this mechanism (continuing a 2016 circular).
  - Restrictions run for one year after removal from the list.

  — [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/india/financial-services/1513042/directions-for-treatment-of-wilful-and-large-defaulters); [The Week, 2024](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2024/07/30/del57-biz-rbi-wilful-defaulters.html); [RBI defaulters' lists](https://www.rbi.org.in/rbidefaulterslist/index.html)
- **Lilavati Trust v. HDFC Bank (Bombay High Court, 2026, interim): a defamation suit that failed.** The trust sued the bank for ₹1,000 crore over public statements calling it a wilful defaulter. The court refused interim restraint, found no prima facie defamation because material on record showed substantial sums due, and ordered the trust to pay ₹5 lakh in costs. Truth of the debt was the defence. — [Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/stocks/bombay-high-court-lilavati-trust-hdfc-bank-defamation-interim-relief-2026/); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/bombay-hc-slams-litigation-stall-hdfc-bank-recovery-imposes-rs-5-lakh-costs-lilavati-trust.html)

### Inferences
- A public "defaulter society" list would sit closest to the trade-association model (FOSTTA, NoDefaulters), but those publish to members or registered users, not the open world. A list on FirsThing's portal that is visible to other societies resembles them; a fully public page goes further than most.
- The common safeguards a listing scheme should copy:
  - an objective threshold (an amount and a number of days overdue);
  - prior notice with a cure period;
  - verification before listing;
  - a right of reply or representation;
  - a documented removal rule, including for how long after payment the entry stays visible.
- The Lilavati case suggests truth of a documented, undisputed debt is the core defence against defamation. Behaviour-based labels (how staff treat field executives) are subjective and far harder to defend than an invoice ageing fact.

### Gaps
- Dun & Bradstreet Paydex was not covered; the search returned nothing on it.
- No primary text of the RBI Master Direction was read, so the exact removal rules are not confirmed.
- No GST-portal analogue of naming defaulters was researched.
- No India builders' or transport association defaulter list was found.
- No court ruling specifically on a trade association's defaulter list was found.
- FOSTTA's removal process and the publication date of its announcement are unconfirmed.

## Indian housing-society apps (MyGate, ADDA, NoBrokerHood etc.): do they rank societies or show resident defaulter lists?

### Takeaway
The society-management apps provide defaulter lists of residents within a society, set up by its committee: dues dashboards, reminders, and in ADDA's case a defaulter list posted as a notice-board announcement. No evidence was found of any app ranking societies against each other or publishing society-level ratings. Courts have pushed back on publicly shaming defaulters in co-operative contexts.

### Cited Findings
- **MyGate.** A committee dashboard shows who is overdue in real time. Automatic reminders go by SMS or WhatsApp before and after the due date. A MyGate blog also discusses penalising late payers and incentivising on-time payers. — [MyGate blog: penalise late payments, incentivise payers](https://mygate.com/blog/innovation/penalise-late-payments-incentivize-payers); [MyGate: accounting challenges](https://mygate.com/blog/housing-society/challenges-in-society-accounting/)
- **ADDA.**
  - The admin app shows residents with pending dues and amounts, and sends reminders to owners or tenants.
  - A support article, "How do I put up the list of Maintenance Fee defaulters as an Announcement?", explains posting the defaulter list to the society notice board, with an option to include a breakup of dues. The page could not be fetched to confirm exactly what is shown.

  — [ADDA support](https://support.adda.io/portal/en/kb/articles/how-do-i-put-up-the-list-of-maintenance-fee-defaulters-on-the-notice-board); [ADDA Admin app](https://apps.apple.com/dk/app/adda-admin/id1049281748)
- **Maharashtra co-operative law.**
  - A "defaulter" (dues unpaid three months after a written demand) matters for disqualification from the managing committee under Section 73CA.
  - Commentary notes a defaulter notice is displayed "for information of members".

  — [Indian Cooperative](https://www.indiancooperative.com/cooperative-queries/query-of-ajay-handoo/)
- **Kerala High Court, Dec 2024: what was displayed.** A co-operative bank displayed defaulting borrowers' names, photos and loan amounts on a flex board. — [SCC Online, 2024](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2024/12/26/borrowers-coerced-repayment-threats-reputational-damage-privacy-invasion-kerala-hc/); [IndiaLaw](https://indialaw.in/blog/civil/public-shaming-loan-defaults-kerala-hc-no)
- **Kerala High Court, Dec 2024: the holding.**
  - The single judge held the Act and Rules do not provide for display as a mode of recovery.
  - Borrowers cannot be coerced into repaying through threats of reputational damage or invasion of their privacy.
  - The judgment linked this to Article 21 dignity.
  - It is a single-bench decision under Kerala co-operative law.

  — [SCC Online, 2024](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2024/12/26/borrowers-coerced-repayment-threats-reputational-damage-privacy-invasion-kerala-hc/); [IndiaLaw](https://indialaw.in/blog/civil/public-shaming-loan-defaults-kerala-hc-no)

### Inferences
- The housing-app precedent is internal: a society shows its own members who owes it. That is a narrower audience than FirsThing showing one society's default to other societies.
- The Kerala ruling is the strongest Indian signal against "shaming as a recovery tool". It concerned individuals and photos, but its reasoning (no statutory basis, coercion through reputational threat) could be argued by a listed society's office-bearers. Societies are associations, not individuals, which may weaken the Article 21 privacy angle, but defamation and the coercion framing still apply.

### Gaps
- NoBrokerHood, Society Notebook and ApnaComplex defaulter features were not researched separately.
- The exact fields ADDA shows (names, flat numbers, amounts) are unconfirmed because the page fetch failed.
- No source found on whether the DPDP Act 2023 restricts publishing named defaulter data; not researched.
- No precedent found of any app or vendor ranking housing societies publicly.

## "Wall of fame" / customer recognition in B2B

### Takeaway
B2B recognition is overwhelmingly opt-in and positive. Vendors celebrate customers through case studies and awards that the customer agrees to and is credited in. The searches surfaced mostly vendor-side awards (Gartner Peer Insights "Customers' Choice"), which shows a review-count plus rating-threshold model for public recognition, rather than any customer-ranking wall.

### Cited Findings
- **Gartner Peer Insights "Customers' Choice".** It recognises vendors with at least 50 reviews and an average of 4.2 stars or more (2018 criteria). Zoho, HubSpot and Salesforce appear in the CRM lists. It is a recognition model built on a minimum evidence count plus a score threshold. — [Solutions Review](https://solutionsreview.com/marketing-automation/gartner-names-december-peer-insight-customer-choice-crm-lead-management); [Zoho blog](https://blog.zoho.com/index.php/crm/april-2019-gartner-peer-insights-customers-choice-for-sfa.html) (dated, 2018–2019)
- **SaaS loyalty and referral programmes.** A Substack post argues for them because SaaS depends on renewals. It is opinion, and the author has not implemented them. — [Mehdeeka Substack](https://mehdeeka.substack.com/p/b2b-loyalty-rewards-programs-saas)
- **Vendor-side award programmes.** Industry awards (Forrester B2B Programs of the Year, the SaaS Awards) recognise vendors or programmes, not individual customers. — [Forrester](https://go.forrester.com/b2b-marketing/award-winners)

### Inferences
- The positive side of FirsThing's idea (star societies) has good precedent if it is framed as opt-in recognition, like case-study consent. It avoids the legal risks of the negative side.
- A minimum-evidence rule like Gartner's (for example, at least N invoices on record before a society can be ranked) is a reusable pattern, so a society with one paid invoice is not crowned a star.

### Gaps
- No concrete examples were found of B2B companies publicly ranking or "starring" their own customers (for example, a customer leaderboard); the searches returned vendor awards instead.
- Customer-advocacy programmes (Salesforce Trailblazer awards, customer-of-the-year awards) were not confirmed with sources.

## Documented backlash, lawsuits or reversals when a company shamed customers

### Takeaway
The clearest Indian precedent is judicial, not commercial: the Kerala High Court (Dec 2024) barred a co-operative from displaying defaulters as a recovery tactic. Conversely, the Bombay High Court (2026, interim) refused to stop a bank calling a trust a wilful defaulter where the debt was documented. No documented case was found of an Indian B2B service company publicly shaming its customers and facing backlash; this is itself a signal that the practice is rare.

### Cited Findings
- **Kerala High Court, Dec 2024.** Publicly displaying defaulters' names, photos and amounts is not a lawful recovery method under the co-operative Act. It amounts to coercion through reputational threat and invades privacy and dignity. — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2024/12/26/borrowers-coerced-repayment-threats-reputational-damage-privacy-invasion-kerala-hc/)
- **Bombay High Court, 2026, interim.** No prima facie defamation where substantial sums due were supported by material on record; costs were awarded against the complainant. — [Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/stocks/bombay-high-court-lilavati-trust-hdfc-bank-defamation-interim-relief-2026/)
- **RBI's own photo-publication rules** are hedged by safeguards: a board-approved, non-discriminatory policy, and publication only after the formal two-committee process. This reflects regulatory caution about naming and shaming. — [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/india/financial-services/1513042/directions-for-treatment-of-wilful-and-large-defaulters)
- **Bombay High Court interim restraints in other defamation cases.** The court has restrained publications it found prima facie false or reckless (Godrej Properties v. Sprouts News; Lodha v. an activist). This shows the speed of interim injunctions against defamatory publication. — [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/high-courts/bombay-high-court/godrej-properties-ltd-vs-ms-sprouts-news-and-anr-restraint-on-publishing-of-defamatory-articles-1589345); [The Goan](https://www.thegoan.net/goa-news/ï»¿realty-firm-files-rs-15-cr-defamation-suit-against-activist/147924.html)

### Inferences
- Legal exposure differs sharply by listing type:
  - a payment-default listing grounded in an invoice and its ageing is defensible on truth;
  - a "behaviour toward field executives" label is opinion-laden and much riskier;
  - a listing used visibly as a lever to force payment invites the "coercion" reasoning of the Kerala ruling.
- Back-office approval of each listing, as FirsThing plans, mirrors RBI's committee review and FOSTTA's verification. Adding a notice-and-cure step and a reply or removal path would match every reputable precedent found.

### Gaps
- No documented commercial backlash cases were found (for example, a SaaS or utility company reversing a customer shame list). The searches did not surface any, and more targeted searching would be needed.
- The UK HMRC "deliberate defaulters" naming scheme (thresholds, duration, deterrent evidence) was identified but could not be read, because the gov.uk fetch failed.
- DPDP Act 2023 implications were not researched.
