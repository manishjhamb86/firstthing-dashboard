# Indian legal limits on publicly naming customers as "defaulters", rating them, or showcasing "star" customers

Scope: India, law as of October 2026. This is research, not legal advice. Every conclusion marked "lawyer to confirm" should be checked by Indian counsel before FirsThing builds or launches a "star / defaulter societies" section. Several primary sources (the RBI site, indiacode, Indian Kanoon full texts) could not be fetched from this environment, so a number of findings rest on secondary summaries. Each one is flagged where that matters.

## 1. Defamation: civil and criminal (IPC 499/500 → BNS s.356); truth, public good, fact vs opinion; Indian defaulter-list cases

### Takeaway
Calling a society a "defaulter" is a statement of fact about its credit. Under s.356's own Explanation 4, an imputation that lowers someone's credit counts as harmful to reputation. The statement is defensible only if it is true, and for the criminal offence also made for the public good. A genuinely disputed, partly paid or wrongly computed invoice makes it risky.

A "worst behaved" ranking built from staff opinion sits closer to comment. Indian courts protect honest comment only when it rests on true facts and is made without malice. A league table published to all other clients, implying misconduct, invites a "facts not proved / malice" attack.

No Indian judgment was found that squarely blesses a private vendor publishing a customer "defaulter list". The closest authorities run both ways:
- banks' accurate statements about real dues are protected;
- public shaming boards were ordered down on privacy and dignity grounds.

### Cited Findings
- **BNS replaced the IPC.** Defamation is now s.356 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, which replaced IPC s.499/500 from 1 July 2024. The definition covers imputations made intending, or with reason to believe, that they will harm reputation, "except in the cases hereinafter excepted" — [devgan.in BNS s.356](https://devgan.in/bns/section/356/); [Vidhi Judicial summary](https://www.vidhijudicial.com/section-356-of-the-bharatiya-nyaya-sanhita-(bns)-of-2023.html)
- **Explanation 4.** An imputation harms reputation if it lowers a person's moral or intellectual character, their standing in their caste or calling, or **their credit**. A "defaulter" label goes directly to credit — [devgan.in BNS s.356](https://devgan.in/bns/section/356/)
- **First exception: truth plus public good.** It is not defamation to impute anything true about a person if it is for the public good that it be published. Whether it is for the public good is a question of fact. These summaries describe the IPC-era wording; BNS carries the exceptions over, but this needs verifying against the Gazette text — [legalserviceindia](https://www.legalserviceindia.com/Legal-Articles/defamation-under-bhartiya-nyaya-sanhita/); [lawfoyer](https://lawfoyer.in/defamation-under-indian-penal-code-and-bhartiya-nyay-sanhita-provisions-exceptions-types-and-cases/)
- **Other exceptions.** They include good-faith opinion on public questions and privileged communications; the summaries also describe a fair-comment / opinion exception — [legalserviceindia](https://www.legalserviceindia.com/Legal-Articles/defamation-under-bhartiya-nyaya-sanhita/)
- **Procedure.** Under s.222 of the BNSS a court ordinarily takes cognizance of s.356 only on a complaint by the aggrieved person, so criminal exposure starts with the society or member filing a complaint — [BNS 356 summary, vidhijudicial](https://www.vidhijudicial.com/section-356-of-the-bharatiya-nyaya-sanhita-(bns)-of-2023.html)
- **Delhi HC: a bank cannot be summoned as an accused for criminal defamation.** A company lacks the mens rea the offence needs. The court quashed complaints against the bank's officers over a "fraud" declaration. This favours FirsThing as a company, but individual employees who publish could still be named — [Verdictum, P.S. Jayakumar v. State, 2025 DHC 9364](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/high-courts/delhi-high-court/p-s-jayakumar-v-state-2025-dhc-9364-defamation-case-summons-against-bank-1596133)
- **Bombay HC (2026), Lilavati Kirtilal Mehta Trust v. HDFC Bank: an accurate statement of dues is protected.** The trust claimed the bank falsely portrayed it as a wilful defaulter. The court refused an interim injunction because the bank's core assertions were supported by material on record, and factual clarifications were not prima facie defamatory. It imposed ₹5 lakh costs — [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/bombay-high-court/lilavati-kirtilal-mehta-trust-v-hdfc-bank-limited-interim-application-no3095-of-2025-media-campaign-not-retaliatory-defamation-1615735); [Kotak Neo news summary](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/stocks/bombay-high-court-lilavati-trust-hdfc-bank-defamation-interim-relief-2026/)
- **Allahabad HC (Rahul Singh): an inaccurate "defaulter" label is exposed.** Bank officials kept showing a borrower as a defaulter on CIBIL after his loan was closed. The court was hearing their plea to quash his defamation complaint and was critical of the bank — [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/allahabad-high-court-asks-icici-bank-chairman-to-explain-use-of-recovery-agents-despite-supreme-court-bar/74247.html)
- **Bombay HC: a bank publishing photographs of a defaulting firm was not stopped.** The court said publication creates awareness and cautions prospective clients. This was in a regulated-bank context (see section 6) — [Moneylife (search summary only; page could not be fetched)](https://www.moneylife.in/article/bank-can-publish-defaulters-photo-high-court/35560.html)
- **Kerala HC, WP(C) 45919 of 2024: a shaming board was ordered down.** A cooperative society put up a flex board listing about 1,750 loan defaulters with photographs. The court upheld the direction to remove it, on the footing that publishing personal information without consent violates privacy and dignity and causes reputational harm — [indialaw.in summary](https://indialaw.in/blog/civil/public-shaming-loan-defaults-kerala-hc-no); [Verdictum PDF](https://verdictum.in/pdf_upload/2157004591920241-578155watermark-1676769.pdf)
- **Housing-society practice.** Maharashtra's Co-operative Societies Act uses "defaulter" as a statutory term (s.73CA) for disqualification from the managing committee. A commentator reads the notice-board display as internal information to members, not a penalty — [indiancooperative.com](https://www.indiancooperative.com/cooperative-queries/query-of-ajay-handoo/). A lawyer answering on a forum said displaying a name as "defaulter" without written notice can amount to defamation; this is opinion, not a holding — [kaanoon / indiancooperative forum](https://www.indiancooperative.com/?p=20196)
- **Malaysia, persuasive only.** In Wong Ming v. Megan Avenue II a management corporation posting owners' dues succeeded on qualified privilege — [burgielaw (search summary)](https://www.burgielaw.com/2023/04/04/can-mc-mc-members-and-pmc-be-held-liable-for-defamation-due-to-publication-of-wrong-list-of-defaulters/)
- **Opinion vs fact, Madras HC.** A Google review calling a lawyer the "worst lawyer" was held not defamatory as an expression of views about services received, under Article 19(1)(a). The court added that false, derogatory statements would be defamation — [Legal500 summary](https://www.legal500.com/developments/?p=40710)
- **Opinion vs fact, Bombay HC (Patel J).** Calling someone out "with fair comment and justification" is not defamation where the criticism is opinion or argument with some basis in fact — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/news-updates/a-statement-is-not-true-only-because-online-bombay-hc-144748)
- **Opinion vs fact, Delhi HC (LawSikho v. Garg).** The court dismissed a company's defamation suit against social-media critics, with ₹1 lakh costs — [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/high-courts/delhi-high-court/addictive-learning-technology-limited-anr-v-aditya-garg-ors-2025-dhc-1178-1569518)

### Inferences
- **"Defaulter".** Defensible only where the amount is undisputed and due under the agreement, the invoice was validly raised and received, and the label is current. A partly paid, disputed, or since-paid invoice turns a true statement into a false one, and the Rahul Singh pattern shows how quickly that becomes actionable.
- **"Overdue" vs "defaulter".** A neutral factual description ("Invoice FT/2026-27/055 of ₹16,579 due 15-08-2026 unpaid as of 21-10-2026") is far safer than the evaluative word "defaulter". In Indian banking "defaulter" carries a regulatory and moral overtone (see section 6).
- **Public good.** The truth defence for the criminal offence also needs public good. Publishing one client's debts to *other* clients, who have no stake in that debt, is weak on public good. A bank warning prospective lenders is a stronger case than a vendor shaming a customer in front of its peers.
- **Behaviour rating.** A "how staff treat our field executives" ranking based on employees' impressions mixes fact ("harassed our technician") with opinion. Implied factual claims must be provable, typically with dated incident records. The ranking format ("worst") signals evaluation and invites a malice argument when it is used alongside collection pressure.
- **Free text.** Free text added by a back-office approver is the highest-risk element, because it is where unprovable factual assertions creep in.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the official BNS Gazette text of the s.356 exceptions; the exception wording above comes from secondary sources.
- No Indian judgment found on a supplier or trade association publishing a customer defaulter list to other customers. The Kerala HC and banking cases are analogies.
- The Bombay HC bank-photo case (Moneylife) could not be opened, so its citation and conditions are unverified.

## 2. Can a registered society / RWA sue for defamation? Can individual committee members?

### Takeaway
Yes, very likely. The statute expressly extends defamation to imputations about a company, an association or a collection of persons, provided it is a definite, identifiable body; a registered society or AOA is clearly that. Individual committee members could also sue if the publication identifies or implicates them, especially if names are shown or the text blames "the committee" or "the secretary".

### Cited Findings
- **Explanation 2 (IPC 499, carried into BNS s.356).** An imputation concerning "a company or an association or collection of persons as such" may amount to defamation — [Indian Kanoon IPC 499](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/10089); [lawgist](https://lawgist.in/indian-penal-code/499)
- **The body must be identifiable.** The Supreme Court in Sahib Singh Mehra read the provision to cover a group of which one "could with certainty say that the particular group had been defamed" (Aligarh prosecuting staff) — [Indian Kanoon](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/39019/); [SCI judgment PDF](https://api.sci.gov.in/jonew/judis/3060.pdf)
- **A shifting group may fail.** The Kerala HC refused to treat the Kerala Police as a determinate body because its membership keeps changing — [Verdictum, Sebastian Paul v. P.R. Ashokan](https://verdictum.in/pdf_upload/sebastian-paul-v-pr-ashokanwatermark-1673633.pdf)
- **Association standing was argued, not decided in the results.** In Crop Care Federation v. Rajasthan Patrika (Delhi HC, 2009) the defendants argued that a pan-India association is not a determinate body. The final ruling was not confirmed in the results — [Columbia Global Freedom of Expression PDF](https://globalfreedomofexpression.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/MS.-CROP-CARE-FEDERATION-OF-INDIA-Vs.-RAJASTHAN-PATRIKA-PVT-LTD.-ORS.pdf); [courtkutchehry](https://courtkutchehry.com/judgments/crop-care-federation-of-india-appellant-hash-rajasthan-patrika-pvt-ltd-and-others-respondent)

### Inferences
- A named housing society with a registration number and a fixed address is a far more determinate body than a nationwide federation or "the police", so standing is unlikely to be a real defence.
- Showing committee members' names next to a "defaulter" or "badly behaved" tag gives each named person an individual claim. They would argue that the non-payment or mistreatment is attributed to their stewardship.

### Gaps
- No Indian case found specifically on an RWA/AOA suing a vendor. The conclusion is drawn from the statute and the identifiable-group cases (lawyer to confirm).

## 3. DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025

### Takeaway
A society as an entity is not a "data principal", so naming the society alone is mostly outside the DPDP Act. Showing committee members' names, photos, or comments attributed to identifiable staff is processing of personal data and needs a lawful basis — in practice consent.

Section 7's "legitimate uses" are narrow and do not obviously cover publishing ratings to other customers. Section 3(c)'s "publicly available" exclusion does not help: committee names in a private contract were not made public by the individuals or under a legal duty.

The Rules were notified on 13 November 2025, with most obligations (notice, security, rights, breach) applying 18 months later, around 13 May 2027.

### Cited Findings
- **Rules notified and phased.** The DPDP Rules 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E)):
  - Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 are in force immediately.
  - Rule 4 (Consent Managers) applies after one year.
  - Rules 3, 5–16 and 22–23 apply after 18 months.
  - The 18-month end date is given as 13 May 2027 by one source and 12 May 2027 by AZB.
  — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1708164/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-notified); [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/); [SS Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-notifies-final-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025/)
- **Data principal.** It is the individual to whom the personal data relates (cited at s.2(j)) — [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-act-publicly-available-personal-data-46899.html/)
- **Rule 3 notice.** A data fiduciary must give the data principal notice to obtain informed consent — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1708164/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-notified)
- **Section 3(c) exclusion.** It covers personal data that the data principal made public, or that another person was legally required to make public. This is narrower than "findable online". The statute's illustration is an individual blogging her views — [dpdpa.com s.3](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-1/section3.html); [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-act-publicly-available-personal-data-46899.html/)
- **Section 7 legitimate uses.** They are a closed list:
  - voluntarily provided data for a specified purpose;
  - State functions;
  - compliance with law or a court order;
  - medical emergencies, epidemics and disasters;
  - employment.

  Commentators call it a renamed "deemed consent" — [Fox Mandal](https://foxmandal.in/indias-new-digital-privacy-law-the-dpdp-act-and-rules-explained/); [JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/commentary/2023/08/indias-data-protection-act-is-more-about-the-processing-of-personal-data-than-it-is-about-privacy/). One blog reads s.7(h) differently; the statute text should be checked.

### Inferences
- **Committee members.** Their names, phone numbers and emails were collected for contract administration (billing, portal access). Using them on a public rating board is a new purpose. Purpose limitation and the consent model mean this would need fresh, specific consent, which members of a "defaulter" society will not give.
- **Positive listings.** Opt-in consent is workable for "star" listings, and should be limited to the society's name, or member names only with each person's own consent.
- **Society-level data.** Name, city and payment record are not personal data in themselves, but identifying details on a small society's page (the secretary's name, a staff member's name in free text) bring the Act back in.
- **Dates.** The core consent and notice obligations apply from mid-2027, but Kerala HC-style privacy and dignity arguments under Article 21 apply now regardless.

### Gaps
- The statute text of s.2(t) ("personal data") and s.7 could not be retrieved directly.
- No Data Protection Board guidance yet on vendor-published customer ratings.

## 4. IT Act / intermediary issues for content on FirsThing's own portal

### Takeaway
FirsThing would be the author and publisher, not an intermediary, so s.79 IT Act safe harbour is unlikely to apply to listings its staff approve and write. The content is published in electronic form and is subject to defamation law like print, as the Madras and Bombay cases on online content show.

### Cited Findings
- **Online statements get no special status.** The Bombay HC held that "a statement is not true merely because it is in print; not false merely because it is online" — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/news-updates/a-statement-is-not-true-only-because-online-bombay-hc-144748)
- **Online statements get no special protection either.** The Madras HC treated a Google review under ordinary defamation principles — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/?p=40710)

### Inferences
- Because a back-office user approves each listing and may add free text, FirsThing exercises editorial control. That is the opposite of an intermediary's passive role.
- Section 79's due-diligence defence is designed for third-party content and is unlikely to apply to the platform's own editorial output (lawyer to confirm; the s.79 text was not retrieved).

### Gaps
- Did not retrieve s.79 or the IT (Intermediary Guidelines) Rules 2021 text. The conclusion above is analytical, not sourced.

## 5. Contract law: confidentiality, consent in agreement or terms of service, disputed invoices

### Takeaway
No source addressed this directly. On ordinary contract principles, publishing a client's payment record and an internal assessment of its staff to other clients is likely to breach any confidentiality clause in the society agreements. Clicking through portal terms is a weak basis for consent to negative publicity.

Disputed invoices make "defaulter" claims especially risky, because a dispute means the debt is not established. The agreement, the GST invoice terms, and any dispute or arbitration clause should be reviewed by counsel.

### Cited Findings
- **Banks win only where the dues are proved.** In Lilavati Trust v. HDFC the bank's assertions were supported by material on record — [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/bombay-high-court/lilavati-kirtilal-mehta-trust-v-hdfc-bank-limited-interim-application-no3095-of-2025-media-campaign-not-retaliatory-defamation-1615735)
- **Where the debt is not established, the label is the risk.** In Rahul Singh the account was closed, so the label was false — [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/allahabad-high-court-asks-icici-bank-chairman-to-explain-use-of-recovery-agents-despite-supreme-court-bar/74247.html)
- **Statutory defaulter regimes require notice.** The Kerala HC board was removed partly because there was no consent to publication — [indialaw.in](https://indialaw.in/blog/civil/public-shaming-loan-defaults-kerala-hc-no)

### Inferences
- **Disputes.** Listing should automatically exclude any invoice with an open dispute, query, credit-note request, or count or savings disagreement. Under FirsThing's own model these disputes are common: invoice light counts vs circuit records, savings basis, and partial payments with TDS.
- **TDS.** A society that has paid net of TDS but whose TDS certificate is not yet recorded is not a defaulter. The listing logic must count TDS as payment.
- **Consent.** If FirsThing wants consent, it should be a specific, separately signed clause in the agreement, not a portal checkbox. Even then it does not waive defamation for false statements.

### Gaps
- No Indian authority found on whether contractual consent covers publishing negative ratings. FirsThing's actual agreement clauses were not reviewed. Lawyer to confirm.

## 6. RBI wilful-defaulter framework and the due-process lessons

### Takeaway
Even regulated lenders, who have a statutory information-sharing mandate, may label and publish a "wilful defaulter" only after a structured process:
- a show-cause notice and the borrower's written reply;
- a reasoned order by an Identification Committee;
- review by a separate Review Committee;
- a board-approved policy governing publication of photographs.

The Supreme Court grounds this in the label's direct and immediate impact on the right to carry on business. A private vendor's "defaulter" tag is a lesser stigma, but courts will look for the same features — notice, a chance to respond, reasons, and an independent review — as evidence of good faith.

### Cited Findings
- **Master Direction.** The RBI Master Direction on Treatment of Wilful Defaulters and Large Defaulters is dated 30 July 2024 and took effect around 28 October 2024. It aims for a "non-discriminatory and transparent procedure … with principles of natural justice" — [Taxmann](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/rbi-issues-master-direction-on-treatment-of-wilful-defaulters-and-large-defaulters); [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/rbis-updated-framework-for-wilful-defaulters-key-provisions-and-impact/); [RBI PDF (could not be fetched here)](https://website.rbi.org.in/documents/87730/39710850/Master+Direction+on+Treatment+of+Wilful+Defaulters+and+Large+Defaulters.pdf)
- **Process steps.** Identification Committee → show-cause notice → the borrower's written submissions → Review Committee. Boards must adopt a policy including guidelines on publishing photographs of wilful defaulters — [probe42 summary](https://resources.probe42.in/regulatory-updates/rbi-circulars/rbi-commercial-banks-treatment-of-wilful-large-defaulters/)
- **Threshold.** The 2023 draft defined a wilful defaulter by outstanding ≥ ₹25 lakh; the draft's review committee was chaired by a whole-time director with two independent directors — [RBI draft notification](https://website.rbi.org.in/web/rbi/-/notifications/draft-master-direction-treatment-of-wilful-defaulters-and-large-defaulters-4318); [Taxmann analysis](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-a-deeper-dive-into-rbis-proposed-norms-for-dealing-with-wilful-defaulters/). The final threshold was not verified here.
- **SBI v. Jah Developers (2019) 6 SCC 787.** Declaring a person a wilful defaulter has a direct and immediate impact on the fundamental right to carry on business, so natural justice applies. The required steps are:
  - a reasoned draft order, served on the borrower;
  - a representation within 15 days;
  - a reasoned order from the Review Committee.
  — [Casemine column](https://www.casemine.com/in/column/the-legal-framework-of-wilful-default-in-india/view); [taxguru (Gujarat HC applying it)](https://taxguru.in/?p=1064434); [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/india/financial-services/1138144/wilful-defaulters-and-intervention-of-courts-should-there-be-a-lakshman-rekha)
- **Unreasoned orders quashed.** The Delhi HC (Frost International v. PNB, 2021) quashed unreasoned orders, and the Bombay HC disfavours non-speaking orders. SBI v. Rajesh Agarwal (SC 2022) read natural justice into the fraud classification — [Casemine](https://www.casemine.com/in/column/the-legal-framework-of-wilful-default-in-india/view); [Legitquest](https://www.legitquest.com/case/frost-international-limited-ors-v-bank-of-baroda/1FB6EF)
- **Credit bureaus.** RBI's own defaulter lists are disseminated through credit information companies (CIBIL), not ad hoc — [RBI press release](https://website.rbi.org.in/hi/web/rbi/-/press-releases/defaulters-lists-now-on-cibil-website-10151)

### Inferences
These features transfer to FirsThing:
1. a written notice to the society naming the invoice, the amount and the date, with at least 15 days to respond;
2. consideration of the reply, with the outcome recorded;
3. a decision by someone other than the collections owner;
4. a reasoned, recorded decision;
5. a materiality threshold;
6. a policy document approved by management;
7. prompt removal on payment or settlement.

Note also that RBI routes defaulter information to *creditors* through credit bureaus, not to the defaulter's peers. That supports keeping any negative information internal.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the final 2024 Master Direction text to quote paragraph numbers, the final threshold, the reply period, or the publication and removal conditions.

## 7. Debt-collection conduct rules: could public naming be coercive collection?

### Takeaway
RBI's recovery-conduct rules bind regulated lenders, not FirsThing, but they show the regulatory and judicial view: humiliation, intimidation and breach of privacy are illegitimate collection tools. The Kerala HC ordered a public defaulter board removed. A portal shaming list visible to a society's peers would very likely be characterised as coercive collection rather than information.

### Cited Findings
- **RBI August 2022 instructions** (banks, NBFCs, ARCs). They bar calling before 8 am or after 7 pm, threats, inappropriate messages and false representations. Regulated entities must ensure that neither they nor their agents use intimidation or harassment, verbal or physical — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/rbi-directs-loan-recovery-agents-not-to-intimidate-borrowers-no-calling-before-8am-after-7pm-1135618.html); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/finance/rbi-directs-loan-recovery-agents-not-to-intimidate-borrowers-no-calling-before-8am-after-7pm-122081201144_1.html); [RBI circular index](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_CircularIndexDisplay.aspx?Id=11937)
- **Recovery-agent codes.** Agents must keep customer information confidential and respect privacy, and the principal is answerable for its agents' conduct — [RBI NBFC notification](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_NBFCNotificationView.aspx?Id=11937)
- **RBI sanctions.** The RBI can bar a bank from using recovery agents in an area for abusive practices (government reply) — [BloombergQuint](https://www.bloombergquint.com/business/no-bank-has-power-to-employ-bouncers-to-recover-loans-govt)
- **Kerala HC.** The court upheld removal of a society's defaulter flex board with photographs as a violation of privacy and dignity; the article is titled "Can public shaming solve loan defaults? Kerala HC says no" — [indialaw.in](https://indialaw.in/blog/civil/public-shaming-loan-defaults-kerala-hc-no)

### Inferences
- Publishing, at the same moment as chasing payment, increases the risk that a court reads the purpose as pressure (malice) rather than the public good. That would defeat the truth-and-public-good and good-faith defences.

### Gaps
- No consumer-protection or general-law authority found applying collection-harassment rules to a non-financial B2B supplier.

## 8. MSMED Act 2006 and MSME Samadhaan / ODR: the lawful channels

### Takeaway
If FirsThing is registered as a micro or small enterprise (Udyam), it has a strong statutory alternative to naming and shaming:
- the buyer must pay within the agreed period, capped at 45 days;
- late payment attracts compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate with monthly rests, which cannot be contracted out of;
- the dispute can be referred to the MSE Facilitation Council for conciliation, then arbitration within 90 days.

Whether an RWA or AOA counts as a "buyer" for MSMED purposes should be confirmed by a lawyer.

### Cited Findings
- **Section 15.** Payment is due by the agreed date, with the credit period capped at 45 days from acceptance; with no agreement, within 15 days — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/law-firms/law-firm-articles-/the-stringency-of-interest-provisions-under-msmed-act-implementation-and-judicial-interpretation-310929); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2022/12/14/when-is-the-interest-rate-payable-under-section-16-of-the-msmed-act-2006-applicable/)
- **Section 16.** Compound interest with monthly rests at three times the bank rate, "notwithstanding anything contained in any agreement" — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/law-firms/law-firm-articles-/the-stringency-of-interest-provisions-under-msmed-act-implementation-and-judicial-interpretation-310929)
- **Sections 17–18.** The buyer is liable for the principal plus interest. A recovery dispute is referred to the MSE Facilitation Council: conciliation, then arbitration, decided within 90 days — [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/?p=130194); [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/labour-employment/mechanism-of-debt-recovery-for-msmes/)
- **Filing route (one source only).** A blog reports that from October 2025 the MSME ODR portal became the route for new delayed-payment references, with Samadhaan kept for pending cases. It also mentions a 2026 amendment that could not be corroborated — [taxguru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1089563)

### Inferences
- Interest accrual stated on invoices and in reminders, plus a Facilitation Council reference, gives FirsThing real leverage with no defamation exposure.
- An existing contractual remedy, CON-13 suspension of servicing after notice, is another lawful lever, provided the agreement provides for it.

## 10. Practical safeguards

### Takeaway
The safeguards below are the ones the courts and the RBI framework reward. Together they convert a high-risk "defaulter" board into a defensible, factual, internal-or-consented disclosure. The safest design omits negative public listing altogether: keep defaulter status internal or between FirsThing and the society itself, and make the public showcase positive-only and opt-in.

### Cited Findings
- **Notice, reply, reasoned order, review** (the RBI and Jah Developers model) — [Casemine](https://www.casemine.com/in/column/the-legal-framework-of-wilful-default-in-india/view); [probe42](https://resources.probe42.in/regulatory-updates/rbi-circulars/rbi-commercial-banks-treatment-of-wilful-large-defaulters/)
- **Accuracy.** Accurate, material-backed statements survive (Lilavati v. HDFC); stale or false ones do not (Rahul Singh) — [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/bombay-high-court/lilavati-kirtilal-mehta-trust-v-hdfc-bank-limited-interim-application-no3095-of-2025-media-campaign-not-retaliatory-defamation-1615735); [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/allahabad-high-court-asks-icici-bank-chairman-to-explain-use-of-recovery-agents-despite-supreme-court-bar/74247.html)
- **Consent and privacy matter**; public shaming boards have been ordered removed — [indialaw.in](https://indialaw.in/blog/civil/public-shaming-loan-defaults-kerala-hc-no)

### Inferences
If FirsThing proceeds, a recommended checklist (to be confirmed by counsel):
1. **Positive-only and opt-in for "stars".** Get written consent from the society (a committee resolution) before naming it, and individual consent before naming any person.
2. **No peer-visible "defaulter" board.** Show each society only its own payment status. If any comparative signal is shown, aggregate and anonymise it ("82% of societies paid on time this quarter").
3. **If negative listing is ever used:**
   - set a materiality threshold;
   - send a written notice with at least 15 days to pay or dispute;
   - exclude any invoice under dispute, query, or credit-note request;
   - treat TDS and part-payment correctly;
   - have someone independent of collections decide, with recorded reasons;
   - remove the listing automatically within 24 hours of payment or settlement;
   - offer a visible right of reply;
   - log every listing.
4. **Factual wording only.** Use "Invoice X of ₹Y due on Z remains unpaid as of D", not "defaulter", "worst", or "badly behaved".
5. **No free-text characterisation of people.** Record staff-conduct issues as dated incidents with evidence, and handle them privately with the committee. Do not publish them as rankings.
6. **No personal data on the board** (DPDP): no committee member names, photos or phone numbers.
7. **Use lawful levers first:** MSMED interest and the Facilitation Council, the contractual suspension clause, and arbitration or civil recovery.
8. **Get counsel's review** of the agreement's confidentiality and dispute clauses before launch.
